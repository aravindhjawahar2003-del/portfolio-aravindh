The Lead Story · Founder’s Edition

He did not wait for a product job. He built a product.

By Aravindh Jawahar · Aspiring Product Manager & Founder · Bengaluru
Aravindh Jawahar — PGDM in Product Management at ISBR Business School, Bengaluru, and the founder of PM Job Getaway.

Every morning, thousands of fresh graduates open job boards full of hope — and close them in frustration. The “entry-level” Product Manager postings they find are expired, duplicated, or were never real openings at all. Aravindh Jawahar lived that frustration himself. He is an aspiring Product Manager pursuing a PGDM in Product Management at ISBR Business School, Bengaluru — and instead of waiting for someone to fix the problem, he decided to become the person who fixes it.

In October 2026, he launched PM Job Getaway — a verified-only board for entry-level Product Management jobs, where every single listing is checked against its original posting and re-checked every 12 hours. If a role closes, it is marked closed. If a link cannot be verified, it does not stay on the board. No expired posts dressed up as fresh ones, no copies of copies — one honest place where a fresher can trust what they see.

That is the spirit running through every page of this portfolio. A commerce graduate from Kumbakonam who taught himself the craft of product management deeply enough to write a 466-page handbook on it. A researcher who has run primary studies on real people and real markets. An author who has written a 50-chapter historical-fantasy novel. The story, as you will read, is only getting started.

About Me

Founder

PM Job Getaway — the board that checks every job, twice a day

Founder & CEO since October 2026 · Bengaluru · pm-launchpad.vercel.app · LinkedIn company page

The mark of the platform — the purple JG monogram of PM Job Getaway, the verified-only board for entry-level Product Management jobs in India and internationally.

Ask any fresher hunting for a first Product Manager role and you will hear the same story. Hours spent applying to postings that quietly expired weeks ago. The same job copied across five sites with five different dates. “Entry-level” roles that demand years of experience once you open them. The search itself had become the biggest obstacle between talented freshers and their first break.

So I built the board I wished existed. PM Job Getaway is a verified-only job board for entry-level Product Management roles in India and internationally, where a job earns its place only after its original posting is checked on the source — and keeps its place only by being re-verified every 12 hours. When a role closes, the board says so. That single promise — every listed role is real and still open — is the whole product, and it is kept every single day.

Running it has been my real-world product management school: prioritising what to build with the same matrices professionals use, designing in Figma, planning in Notion and Jira, mapping flows in Miro, tracking everything in Sheets and Excel, and automating the monitoring with n8n and Claude so no closed job ever lingers unnoticed.

The process — five steps, every job, every time

No job reaches the board on trust. Each one walks the same five steps, and the fifth step never ends.

  1. Discover

    Collect openings from 259 sources — company portals and hiring platforms, India and international.

  2. Verify

    Open the original posting on the source itself and confirm the role is genuinely live.

  3. Review

    Confirm it is a genuine entry-level PM role — the right level, the right function.

  4. Publish

    List it on the India or the International page, newest first.

  5. Monitor

    Re-verify every 12 hours; the moment a role closes, it is closed or updated on the board.

Also Built · The Handbook

The Complete Product Management Knowledge Handbook

Before running a product, I wrote the book on running one. The Complete Product Management Knowledge Handbook runs to 466 pages — the whole craft of product management, organised into six chapters so that any topic can be found, understood and applied:

  • Chapter 1 — Topics & Headings: the complete map of product management, every subject laid out in order, from fundamentals and the product lifecycle to strategy and roadmaps.
  • Chapter 2 — PM Skills: the skills a product manager is expected to practise — discovery, user research, communication, prioritisation and delivery.
  • Chapter 3 — Matrices: the decision grids used on real products, including RICE, ICE, MoSCoW, Kano and SWOT.
  • Chapter 4 — Frameworks: the working frameworks behind strategy, growth, retention and experimentation.
  • Chapter 5 — Formulas: the numbers of the craft — market sizing (TAM–SAM–SOM), activation, retention and the metrics a PM is judged by, each with its formula.
  • Chapter 6 — Processes: how work actually flows — PRD writing, MVP scoping, backlog, sprints and delivery, step by step.

It is the reference the platform is run from, and proof that the craft was learned deeply, not skimmed. The full handbook is attached below.

The Author’s Desk

Ranavettaiyan — a thousand-year-old secret, told in three languages

Between the spreadsheets and the sprint boards, there is another desk in my life — the writer’s. Ranavettaiyan is my historical-fantasy novel: a story that begins with college lovers in the present day uncovering the history of a village samadhi statue, and reaches back a thousand years — to a Sindhu king who sends his two sons out into the world as commoners, to return only when they can raise an army.

The novel exists in Tamil, English and Hindi. The writing is done — all 50 chapters, with a complete Story Bible holding every thread in place, and a second book already hinted at in the final pages. It is not published yet: the editing work is in progress, sharpening every chapter before it meets its readers.

Why does a novel belong in a product portfolio? Because storytelling is the product manager’s oldest tool. A roadmap is a story about the future. A pitch is a story about a problem. Fifty chapters taught me patience, structure and the discipline of finishing something enormous — the same discipline that re-verifies a job board every twelve hours.

Project & Research

Blueprints, bottles and questions asked properly

Two product case studies built the PM way — problem first, roadmap second, measurement always — and a research shelf that runs from completed studies to live primary research happening right now.

2026 · PM Case Study / Blueprint

NegotiateTech — the price is a conversation

Walk into any Indian bazaar and the first price is never the final price — bargaining is a national sport. Online electronics shopping killed that sport: one fixed price, take it or leave it. NegotiateTech, my product blueprint for shoppers aged 20–35, brings the conversation back with a Direct Bargaining Engine — the buyer makes an offer, the seller counters, and both sides walk away with a deal they chose.

The roadmap is phased the way real products grow: first an MVP with negotiation chat, then AI price suggestions that help both sides meet in the middle, then seller analytics and trust scoring. And because a blueprint must know how it will be judged, it carries a measurement plan — activation, 7 and 30-day retention, DAU/MAU and CSAT/NPS. These are planned metrics, the yardsticks chosen in advance — this is a case study and blueprint, not a launched product, and it is presented honestly as one.

What it taught me: marketplace thinking, two-sided incentives, and funnel analysis — my product management and e-commerce coursework applied to a negotiation table that never closes.

2026 · Team Case Study · Team of 8

Nutri Twist — one twist, instant energy

Protein shakes fail at the same moment every day: the messy mixing. Nutri Twist — built by our team of eight, never a solo act — stores the powder in the bottle’s cap. One twist, and the powder meets the water exactly when you want it: “One Twist Instant Energy.”

With HealthKart as collaborator, we positioned it lifestyle-first — “Daily Energy Nutrition” for ages 18–35, sold as a daily habit rather than a gym supplement. Our go-to-market plan reached customers where they already live: Instagram influencers, retail shelves, digital ads and e-commerce.

What it taught me: consumer-product thinking, positioning, and GTM planning as a team sport — eight people, one roadmap, and the marketing subjects from class turned into a launch plan with real channels and real audiences.

Milestones

Eight stories that built the person

Before the titles and the metrics, there were moments. Each one left behind a skill — and a little more steel.

1

The sports field came first

Running, football, long jump, relay, marathon — school sports were my first arena, and I won there. Growing up a sports person made me mentally and physically strong from an early stage: when things turn tough — a closing deadline, a broken launch, a hard room — I have been trained since childhood to stay on my feet and finish the race.

2

A year as Class Representative

For one full year at Guru Nanak College, I stood between the students and the staff — carrying messages both ways, coordinating people with very different expectations, and keeping a whole class moving together. I learned people-handling, clear communication, coordination, leadership and adaptability — sometimes teaching, sometimes delivering the motivational speech that got everyone through a hard week.

3

The wedding studio — where editing work became experience

For a few months I worked in a wedding studio, where other people’s most precious memories passed through my hands. I edited photos, videos, flex designs and posters — completing 10+ individual wedding albums, whole photos and videos, plus videos for YouTube channels. The studio paid me in two currencies: real earnings, and editing craft I still use whenever a product needs a story told visually.

4

The face of my college

As ISBR’s Website Ambassador, I represented my college to the world outside it — creating awareness, speaking with students who were searching for the right college and with those about to join, and contributing back to the institution that was building me. More than 10 students joined through that influence. Connection building, marketing and honest awareness — learned by standing in front of strangers and earning their trust.

5

Six events, start to finish

Startups events, founder meetups and competitions — I organised and conducted six events at my college and made each one land successfully. Budget collection, roadmapping the event, coordinating teams, carrying responsibility when something wobbled: event organising taught me how anything — including a product launch — is actually done properly.

6

Three companies trusted my prototypes

Three companies received working prototypes from my desk — a website, an app and a dashboard — built with Figma, user storytelling and the right tools for each problem. A student handing a company a prototype it can react to is a student who has stopped asking for permission to be technical. This is where I became technically strong.

7

Pitch rooms — and a government school classroom

I have pitched my startup ideas, prototype in hand, in 3+ startup competitions — reaching the finalist stage in some — learning pitch, presentation, marketing and the art of the idea itself. And through NGO teaching, I stood in a government school classroom before students of Classes 3 to 10, creating awareness about startups and entrepreneurship. Communication, teaching and social contribution: the pitch persuades a jury, but the classroom teaches you why the work matters.

8

The bug reports companies actually fixed

I have a habit: when an app or website fails its users, I do not just close it — I find the problem properly, work out a solution, and mail it to the company. Big companies, medium ones, small ones — many of them corrected their app or website based on what I sent. Analysis, problem-finding and solutions, practised on live products long before I had the title. That habit, scaled up, became PM Job Getaway.
